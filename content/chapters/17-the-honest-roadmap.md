# Chapter 17: The Honest Roadmap — Communicating What's Not Built Yet

There's a version of a roadmap that exists to make a product look further along than it is, and a version that exists to tell the truth about where it actually stands. The first kind erodes on contact with any real scrutiny — a single pointed question about a listed feature reveals it doesn't exist, and every other claim on the page loses credibility along with it. The second kind is the only one that survives being looked at closely, which is the entire audience this manual and this portfolio are written for.

## The rule this whole build ships under

Every README in this portfolio ends with a status line that says exactly what's true, not what would sound better: live or in progress, solo build, the month, and — for this manual specifically — how many of seventeen chapters are actually written. Not "coming soon" with no number attached, not a vague "actively developing," a specific, checkable fraction. That specificity is the whole point. A vague roadmap can't be caught being wrong, because it never committed to anything checkable in the first place. A specific one can be, which is exactly why it's more credible, not less — it's a claim that invites verification instead of quietly avoiding it.

## This manual is the roadmap principle applied to itself

This chapter is being written as chapter seventeen of seventeen, which means the shipping pattern for the whole manual was decided before a majority of it existed: publish a real subset live, mark the rest "Coming" in the table of contents rather than hide the gap or fill it with placeholder text that reads like content but says nothing. That's not a compromise made because finishing all seventeen chapters at once wasn't feasible — it's the deliberate choice described back in Chapter 6, the same "ship incomplete-but-honest" instinct applied to the manual's own existence, not just to the products it describes. A reader landing on the table of contents today sees precisely which chapters are real and which aren't yet, with nothing dressed up to look more finished than it is.

## Why an empty section is more credible than a padded one

The instinct to fill every section of a README or roadmap — even with something thin — comes from a reasonable place: an empty section looks unfinished, and unfinished feels like a weakness to hide. But a thin, padded-out section is worse than an empty one, not better, because it fails differently: it fails on close reading instead of at a glance, after a reviewer has already invested the time to read it and feels misled by what they find. The empty numbers table from Chapter 13, the "Coming" chapters in this manual's own table of contents, the README status line that says "in progress" instead of overstating readiness — all of it fails the same test in the same honest way: nothing there claims more than it can back up, so there's nothing for a skeptical read to catch.

## What a roadmap is actually for, once you strip away the performance

A roadmap that's honestly incomplete does something a padded one can't: it tells a technical reviewer exactly how to calibrate everything else on the page. Six of seventeen chapters live, clearly marked, means the reviewer knows precisely how much of this manual to weigh, and — because it was stated plainly rather than obscured — trusts the six that exist considerably more than they would if the other eleven had been quietly implied to exist too. Honesty about what's missing is what makes the rest of the claim believable. That trade — a roadmap that looks less finished in exchange for one that's actually trusted — is the correct trade every time, and it's the one this entire portfolio, chapter by chapter and product by product, has been built to make.
